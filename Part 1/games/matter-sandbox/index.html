Survivor controls

A/D or ←/→ walk, W or ↑ jump, F swing at nearby zombies. Getting bitten turns you into one of them. In water you sink slowly instead of falling — tap W repeatedly to paddle back up to the surface, and staying fully submerged too long will drown you. Paint a Recruit to hand control to a new body — your old one wanders off as an ordinary human.

Field notes

Fire ignites wood, oil, plant and gunpowder, dies in water. Acid eats stone, sand, wood, plant and gunpowder. Bombs and gunpowder detonate on contact with flame and chain into their own kind. Lava incinerates fuel, turns sand to glass, melts snow and ice, and hardens to stone in water. Snow melts near heat; water freezes where it touches ice. Plant grows toward water. Fuse carries flame fast and true — wire it to a bomb. Clone copies whatever last touched it; Void erases anything it touches. Zombies hunt down humans but prefer chasing you, and spread the infection on contact; cure turns them back. Painting glass over anything seals it behind the pane, visible but frozen in place — until an explosion shatters the glass and spills it back out. Sand turns to mud where it touches standing water; concrete sets into stone the same way. Crystal is tough and inert but still melts against lava; honey is a slow, syrupy liquid that smothers flame without boiling away.

Reactions

Sodium bursts into flame — sometimes a small blast — the instant it touches water. Base neutralizes acid on contact, leaving water and a little salt. Baking soda fizzes into foam when it meets acid; yeast and peroxide do the same, more violently. Ammonia and bleach combine into a lethal toxic gas — never mix them. Thermite ignites into molten metal that burns straight through stone. Iron rusts in standing water and melts in fire or lava. Napalm catches fire far more readily than oil and keeps burning long after — even sitting on water. A spark instantly detonates or ignites whatever it touches, then vanishes. Oxygen feeds any flame it drifts into, burning brighter and longer. Nitroglycerin is wildly unstable — the faintest touch of flame or acid sets it off, and it can go off on its own.

Devices

Dry ice slowly sublimates into fog, faster near heat or water. Paint a blue and an orange portal — sized by your brush — and anything that touches one steps out the other, you and every human or zombie included; placing a new one retires its predecessor. A fan blows whatever sits above it further up. Heaters and coolers radiate warmth or cold into anything nearby. Water and lava sources trickle out their material forever.
